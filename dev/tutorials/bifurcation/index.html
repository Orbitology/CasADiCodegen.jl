Bifurcation diagrams

The steady states of a model change with its parameters, and at some values of a parameter they change in number or in stability: a steady state splits into three, or loses its stability while a periodic orbit appears around it. A bifurcation diagram shows these changes, and BifurcationKit.jl computes it by continuation, which follows a branch of steady states as the parameter moves, detects the special points on it and starts the new branches that leave them. The method needs the residual of the equations and its Jacobian at each step, and derivatives of a higher order at the special points. In this tutorial, we give BifurcationKit.jl a CasADi function, so that all these derivatives are generated code, and we select the continued parameter and the quantities that the branches record by their names. We compute the diagram of a pitchfork, then the Hopf point of a system of differential equations and the branch of periodic orbits that starts there.

The examples need CasADiCodegen.jl and BifurcationKit.jl; using BifurcationKit also loads SciMLBase.jl, on which the method of this tutorial depends.

using CasADiCodegen
using BifurcationKit

The bifurcation problem of a CasADi function

As a first example, we take the steady states of a system of two states $x$ and $y$ and two parameters $\mu$ and $\alpha$:

\[\begin{aligned} 0 &= \mu x - x^3 + \alpha y, \\ 0 &= -y. \end{aligned}\]

The state $x = y = 0$ is a steady state for every $\mu$, and for $\mu > 0$ there are two more, $x = \pm\sqrt{\mu}$ with $y = 0$. As for a NonlinearFunction (see Solve a system of nonlinear equations), the CasADi function takes the state and the parameters, in this order, and its first result is the residual. The states and the parameters take the names of their scalar symbols, here x, y, μ and α; the elements of a symbol of several elements, such as MX.sym("u", 2), are u_0 and u_1. Functions of SX and of MX symbols both serve:

x, y, μ, α = SX.sym("x"), SX.sym("y"), SX.sym("μ"), SX.sym("α")
pitchfork = CasadiFunction("pitchfork", [vcat(x, y), vcat(μ, α)], [vcat(μ * x - x^3 + α * y, -y)])

BifurcationProblem takes the function, a guess of a steady state, the values of the parameters and the name of the parameter that the continuation varies, here $\mu$, while $\alpha$ keeps its value; :α, or its place 2, would vary $\alpha$ instead. The guess and the values are vectors in the order of the symbols, or, as here, pairs of a name and a value, one for each element. The guess need not be a steady state, since the continuation first corrects it by Newton's method. The keyword record_from_solution names what each point of a branch records, here the state $x$:

problem = BifurcationProblem(pitchfork, [:x => 1.0, :y => 1.0], [:μ => -1.0, :α => 1.0], :μ;
                             record_from_solution = :x)

The problem holds generated code for the residual and its Jacobian, and for the derivatives that BifurcationKit.jl uses at special points: the derivatives of the second and third order by the state in given directions, and the derivative of the residual and of its Jacobian by the continued parameter, besides the product of the Jacobian with a vector. CasADi computes all of them from the graph of the residual. With jet = false the problem holds the residual and the Jacobian only, which takes less code to generate and to compile, and BifurcationKit.jl differentiates the generated residual itself, by automatic differentiation and, for the derivative of the Jacobian by the parameter, by finite differences.

The diagram

The settings of the continuation are a ContinuationPar, which holds the interval of the parameter and the number of eigenvalues that each step computes, from which the continuation detects a change of stability. bifurcationdiagram follows the branch from the guess, and from each branch point it starts the new branches, here to a depth of two levels and on both sides of each point. Its fourth argument is a function that returns the settings of each level, here the same ones. The result is a tree: diagram.γ is the first branch, and diagram.child holds the diagrams of the branches that start on it:

options = ContinuationPar(nev = 2, p_min = -4.0, p_max = 6.0)
diagram = bifurcationdiagram(problem, PALC(), 2, (args...) -> options; bothside = Val(true))
[(point.type, point.param) for point in diagram.γ.specialpoint]
3-element Vector{Tuple{Symbol, Float64}}:
 (:endpoint, -4.0)
 (:bp, 0.0004456099444045391)
 (:endpoint, 6.0)

The first branch, $x = 0$, has a branch point (bp) near $\mu = 0$ between its two end points. The continuation locates the point by bisection to the precision of its settings, a few times $10^{-4}$ here, which is why it is not exactly at zero. The branch that starts at the point is $x = \pm\sqrt{\mu}$, which the diagram finds twice, once in each direction from the point. Since each side of it continues to the end of the interval, $\mu = 6$, and the branch holds its two sides one after the other, it runs from one end, $x = -\sqrt{6}$ or $x = \sqrt{6}$, through the branch point to the other. Each point records $x$ as its named field:

[(branch.γ.x[1], branch.γ.x[end]) for branch in diagram.child], sqrt(6)
([(-2.4494897427831788, 2.4494897427831783), (2.4494897427831788, -2.4494897427831783)], 2.449489742783178)

BifurcationKit.jl draws a diagram with Plots.jl or Makie.jl, plot(diagram) once one of them is loaded.

A Hopf point and periodic orbits

A steady state of a system of differential equations can also lose its stability to a periodic orbit. The system

\[\begin{aligned} x' &= \mu x - y - x (x^2 + y^2), \\ y' &= x + \mu y - y (x^2 + y^2) \end{aligned}\]

has the steady state $x = y = 0$ for every $\mu$, which is stable for $\mu < 0$ and unstable for $\mu > 0$; for $\mu > 0$ the circle of radius $\sqrt{\mu}$ is a periodic orbit. We write its right side as a CasADi function f(u, p, t), the form that an ODEFunction takes, and give it to BifurcationProblem, which accepts the time as a third argument when the residual does not depend on it; a residual that depends on the time has no steady states, and BifurcationProblem rejects it with an ArgumentError. The second result, the radius $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, is an observed variable, which the problem knows by the name of the result, radius, while the states keep the names of their symbols, u and v:

u, v, ν, t = SX.sym("u"), SX.sym("v"), SX.sym("ν"), SX.sym("t")
r2 = u^2 + v^2
oscillator = CasadiFunction("oscillator", [vcat(u, v), ν, t], [vcat(ν * u - v - u * r2, u + ν * v - v * r2), sqrt(r2)],
                            ["state", "ν", "t"], ["rate", "radius"])
problem = BifurcationProblem(oscillator, [0.0, 0.0], [1.0], :ν; record_from_solution = (:u, :radius))
options = ContinuationPar(nev = 2, p_min = -3.0, p_max = 3.0)
branch = continuation(problem, PALC(), options; bothside = Val(true))
[(point.type, point.param) for point in branch.specialpoint]
3-element Vector{Tuple{Symbol, Float64}}:
 (:endpoint, -3.0)
 (:hopf, -0.0004456099444045391)
 (:endpoint, 3.0)

The record holds both names, branch.u and branch.radius, which are zero along the whole branch, since the steady state stays at the origin. The continuation also counts the unstable eigenvalues of the Jacobian at each point; the branch runs from $\nu = -3$ to $\nu = 3$, and it has two of them on the side of $\nu > 0$:

extrema(branch.radius), branch.n_unstable[1], branch.n_unstable[end]
((0.0, 0.0), 0, 2)

From the Hopf point, the special point 2 in the list above, continuation starts the branch of periodic orbits, which it computes by collocation (Collocation of BifurcationKit.jl) on 20 intervals with polynomials of degree 5. get_periodic_orbit returns the orbit at point 10 of the branch, whose row i holds state i at the times orbit.t; the largest value of $u$ is the radius of the circle:

orbits = continuation(branch, 2, options, Collocation(20, 5))
orbit = get_periodic_orbit(orbits, 10)
maximum(orbit[1, :]), sqrt(orbits.param[10])
(0.6749127632744238, 0.6749127632742167)

The two agree to about $10^{-13}$.
