Change the independent variable

A differential equation is usually written in the variable in which its laws are stated, most often the time, but another variable can serve the purpose of a simulation better: the horizontal distance of a projectile when the question is where it lands, or the logarithm of a scale factor when the solution changes over many orders of magnitude. When the derivative $f_s$ of one state $s$ of $x' = f(x, t)$ has no zero on the interval of the solution, $s$ is strictly monotonic in time and can replace it, and the chain rule gives the equations of the other states in $s$,

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}x_j}{\mathrm{d}s} = \frac{f_j(x, t)}{f_s(x, t)}, \qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}s} = \frac{1}{f_s(x, t)},\]

and the time becomes a state whose equation follows from the rule for the derivative of an inverse function. In this tutorial, we write this rewriting as a few operations on CasADi expressions, solve a projectile in its horizontal distance, and then integrate a cosmological model in the logarithm of its scale factor, where the interval of the original time is not known in advance. The examples need CasADiCodegen.jl, SciMLBase.jl and OrdinaryDiffEqTsit5.jl.

The change of variable on expressions

A CasADi model holds its right side as an expression of the symbols of the state, so the chain rule above is a division of expressions. The function below takes the state and the right side as CasADi columns, the index i of the new independent variable in the state, and the old independent variable t. It returns the pair (z, dz): the new state z, which is the state without its element i, followed by the old variable t, and the derivative dz of z by the element i, which becomes the independent variable. The symbol t must be the one that the right side uses; for an autonomous model, a new symbol serves:

using CasADiCodegen
using OrdinaryDiffEqTsit5: Tsit5
import SciMLBase

function change_independent_variable(state, rhs, i, t)
    n = size(state, 1)
    @assert size(rhs, 1) == n && 1 <= i <= n
    keep = [j for j in 1:n if j != i]
    return vcat(state[keep], t), vcat(rhs[keep] / rhs[i], 1 / rhs[i])
end

The right side dz still contains the symbol state[i] where rhs contained it, so that symbol is the third argument, the time, of the CasADi function that we give to ODEFunction.

A projectile in its horizontal distance

A ball is thrown at 45 degrees in the gravity $g$, so that its horizontal and vertical velocities are both $v$ at the start. Its position $(x, y)$ and its vertical velocity $v_y$ follow

\[\dot{x} = v, \qquad \dot{y} = v_y, \qquad \dot{v}_y = -g,\]

from $x = y = 0$ and $v_y(0) = v$. To know the height of the ball at a target 10 m away, we take $x$ as the independent variable, which is possible because $\dot{x} = v$ is never zero, and get the state $(y, v_y, t)$ as a function of $x$:

x, y, vy, t = SX.sym("x"), SX.sym("y"), SX.sym("vy"), SX.sym("t")
g, v = SX.sym("g"), SX.sym("v")
z, dz = change_independent_variable(vcat(x, y, vy), vcat(v, vy, -g), 1, t)
ball = CasadiFunction("ball", [z, vcat(g, v), x], [dz])
problem = SciMLBase.ODEProblem(SciMLBase.ODEFunction(ball), [0.0, 8.0, 0.0], (0.0, 10.0), [9.81, 8.0])
solution = SciMLBase.solve(problem, Tsit5())
solution[[:y, :t]][end]
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 2.3359375000000213
 1.2499999999999996

The span of the problem is now a span of distance, from 0 to 10 m, so that the solve stops at the target, and the solution interpolates the height at any distance in between, for instance solution(5.0). The exact values at 10 m are the time of flight $t = x / v = 1.25$ s and the height $y = v t - g t^2 / 2 = 2.3359375$ m, which the solver reproduces to rounding, since the solution is a polynomial of second degree in $x$.

In the time, the same question needs an event that stops the solver where $x$ reaches 10 m. A ContinuousCallback of SciMLBase.jl with the condition $x - 10$ and the affect terminate! does this:

in_time = CasadiFunction("ball_t", [vcat(x, y, vy), vcat(g, v), t], [vcat(v, vy, -g)])
stop = SciMLBase.ContinuousCallback((u, t, integrator) -> u[1] - 10, SciMLBase.terminate!)
problem_t = SciMLBase.ODEProblem(SciMLBase.ODEFunction(in_time), [0.0, 0.0, 8.0], (0.0, 5.0), [9.81, 8.0])
solution_t = SciMLBase.solve(problem_t, Tsit5(); callback = stop)
solution_t.t[end], solution_t[:y][end]
(1.25, 2.335937499999885)

Both routes give the same answer here. The change of variable avoids the root finding of the event, and it gives the trajectory as a function of the distance, which suits a question about a place, while the event suits a single crossing.

A cosmological model in logarithmic time

In cosmology, the Friedmann equations describe the expansion of the universe through its scale factor $a$, the size of the universe relative to today, so that $a = 1$ today and $a = 0$ at its beginning. Each species has a density $\Omega_s$, in units of the total density today, which varies as $a^{-3(1 + w_s)}$ when the universe expands, with $w = 1/3$ for radiation, $0$ for matter and $-1$ for dark energy. In the conformal time $t$, whose increment is that of the cosmic time divided by $a$, in units of the inverse of today's rate of expansion, and with $\Omega$ the sum of the densities,

\[a' = \sqrt{\Omega}\, a^2, \qquad \Omega_s' = -3 (1 + w_s) \frac{a'}{a} \Omega_s .\]

Integrated backward from today, the scale factor reaches zero at a finite conformal time, where the densities diverge, and this time is not known before the solve. An interval that reaches past it has no solution at its end: the solver takes ever smaller steps as it approaches the singularity, and stops with the code Unstable and a warning, which we silence with the logger of the standard library Logging:

using Logging: with_logger, NullLogger

Ωs, a = SX.sym("Ω", 3), SX.sym("a")
w = [1 / 3, 0, -1]
da = sqrt(sum(Ωs[k] for k in 1:3)) * a^2
dΩ = vcat((-3 * (1 + w[k]) * da / a * Ωs[k] for k in 1:3)...)
friedmann = CasadiFunction("friedmann", [vcat(Ωs, a)], [vcat(dΩ, da)])
today = [5e-5, 0.3, 1 - 5e-5 - 0.3, 1.0]                  # Ω_r, Ω_m, Ω_Λ, a
problem = SciMLBase.ODEProblem(SciMLBase.ODEFunction(friedmann), today, (0.0, -3.3))
solution = with_logger(() -> SciMLBase.solve(problem, Tsit5(); reltol = 1e-5), NullLogger())
solution.retcode, solution.t[end]
(SciMLBase.ReturnCode.Unstable, -3.258377373623186)

The scale factor grows monotonically with the time when $\Omega > 0$, so it can replace the time, and an interval of the scale factor, unlike one of the time, can be chosen to end before the beginning of the universe. Its logarithm $b = \ln a$, which spreads the orders of magnitude evenly, can replace it in turn. We change from $t$ to $a$ with the function above, which keeps the conformal time as the fourth state, and then from $a$ to $b$: with $a = e^b$ and $\mathrm{d}a / \mathrm{d}b = e^b$, the derivative by $b$ is the derivative by $a$ with $a$ replaced by $e^b$, times $e^b$. substitute makes the replacement in the expression:

z, dz_a = change_independent_variable(vcat(Ωs, a), vcat(dΩ, da), 4, SX.sym("t"))
b = SX.sym("b")
dz_b = substitute(dz_a, a, exp(b)) * exp(b)

The new right side depends on $b$ itself, which is therefore the time argument of the function. ODEFunction takes the time as the third argument, after the parameters, and this model has none, so an empty column of parameters, SX(0, 1), holds their place, and the problem gets no parameters:

friedmann_b = CasadiFunction("friedmann_b", [z, SX(0, 1), b], [dz_b])
problem_b = SciMLBase.ODEProblem(SciMLBase.ODEFunction(friedmann_b), [today[1:3]; 0.0], (0.0, -15.0))
solution_b = SciMLBase.solve(problem_b, Tsit5(); reltol = 1e-8)
solution_b.retcode, solution_b.t[end]
(SciMLBase.ReturnCode.Success, -15.0)

The solver reaches $b = -15$, a scale factor of $3 \times 10^{-7}$, since the interval ends before the singularity; we take a tight one to compare with the exact solution. In $b$, the equations of the densities are linear, $\mathrm{d}\Omega_s / \mathrm{d}b = -3 (1 + w_s) \Omega_s$, with the exact solution $\Omega_s(b) = \Omega_s(0)\, e^{-3 (1 + w_s) b}$, which explains why they vary so evenly. The largest relative difference of the solution from it at the end is

exact = [today[k] * exp(-3 * (1 + w[k]) * -15.0) for k in 1:3]
maximum(abs, solution_b.u[end][1:3] ./ exact .- 1)
4.188615354561165e-8

The fourth state is the conformal time. It changes by 3 between $b = -5$ and $b = 0$, but by less than 0.04 between $b = -15$ and $b = -9$, where radiation dominates, so that this era, which the solve in $b$ resolves in evenly spaced steps, occupies a tiny interval of conformal time just after $a = 0$, near $t = -3.2584$, where the solve in $t$ stopped:

[(bk, solution_b(bk)[4]) for bk in (-15.0, -9.0, -5.0, 0.0)]
4-element Vector{Tuple{Float64, Float64}}:
 (-15.0, -3.2583165974548196)
 (-9.0, -3.243309565574223)
 (-5.0, -3.00208389879915)
 (0.0, 0.0)
